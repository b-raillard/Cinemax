## Design System (all RULE — `docs/design-system/conventions.md` rejection checklist is authoritative; read `docs/design-system/README.md` before editing UI)

- Tokens in `CinemaGlassTheme.swift`. All `CinemaColor` use `Color.dynamic(light:dark:)`. **Never `Color(hex:)` for new tokens.**
- **Shared toggle**: `CinemaToggleIndicator` (Capsule+Circle pill), parent-driven (`Button { value.toggle() }`). Never system `Toggle` in settings.
- **No 1px borders** — use color shifts. Glass panels: `.glassPanel()`.
- **Accent**: `themeManager.accent`/`.accentContainer`/`.accentDim`/`.onAccent` — never `CinemaColor.tertiary*`. All dual-mode.
- **Dark/Light**: always route through `themeManager.darkModeEnabled =` / `themeManager.accentColorKey =` — direct `@AppStorage` writes bypass `_accentRevision` and break reactivity. **RULE — the rainbow tick bumps `_rainbowTick`, never `_accentRevision`**, and only the rainbow branch of the accent getters reads it: sharing the counter made `colorScheme` re-run the root `AppNavigation.body` ten times a second (lot 8, P7 — 2,989 → 1,355 main-thread samples per 15 s on the A15). Locked by `ThemeManagerRainbowTickTests`. `.preferredColorScheme()` applied at root in `AppNavigation` only.
- **Hardcoded `.white`/`.black`**: only inside the video player (always dark). Else `CinemaColor.onSurface`/`.onSurfaceVariant`.
- **RULE — anything drawn ON `accentContainer` (CTA label, selected chip / season tab, first settings pill, a sheet's accent ✕) takes `themeManager.onAccentContainer`, never `.white` and never `onAccent`** (audit 2026-09-22, U4). White on the container measured 2.62:1 on yellow, 2.77:1 on cyan, 3.36:1 on orange — under WCAG's 3:1 even for large text on the first two, on « Lecture » and « Connexion ». `AccentLabelContrast` (`AccentOption.swift`, pure, locked by `AccentLabelContrastTests`) keeps white unless it drops under 3.5:1 AND near-black `#1A1A1A` reads better — i.e. orange / yellow / cyan go dark (5.2–6.6:1), the saturated reds, greens, blues, indigo, purple and pink keep the design's white (≥ 3:1). Numeric rather than a per-case list, so the RAINBOW accent — whose hue sweeps through yellow and cyan — gets the same answer frame by frame. `onAccent` stays what it was: the partner of `accent` (the lighter tone, near-black in dark mode) for chips filled with `accent`.
- **Font scaling**: `CinemaScale.factor` = 1.4× base on tvOS × user `uiScale` (80–130%). **RULE — no bare `.font(.system(size: N))` numeric literals** — wrap `N` in `CinemaScale.pt(...)` or use a `CinemaFont.*` token, else it ignores `uiScale`/tvOS 1.4×. Exception: Play/Lecture labels hardcode 28pt on tvOS (computed var, not a bare literal).
- **tvOS focus**: `@FocusState` + `.focusEffectDisabled()` + `.hoverEffectDisabled()`, no white bg. Indicator = **3px accent `strokeBorder` + accent-tinted halo** (`cinemaFocus()`, was 2px@0.8 + a near-invisible grey glow). Cards **scale 1.06** + brightness on focus (`CinemaTVCardButtonStyle`); CTAs (`CinemaTVButtonStyle`) add an **accent focus ring + 3pt lift + ghost-fill brighten**; round portraits (`CastCircle`) draw their **own circular** accent ring (the rectangular `cinemaFocus()` is wrong for a circle). **RULE — the 1.06 card growth (~8pt on the 6-col/32pt-gutter tvOS grid, single-item focus) never overlaps neighbours, but every card-bearing scroll row MUST keep `.scrollClipDisabled()`** so the scaled edge card isn't clipped. Opt-in `dimUnfocusedPosters` (default off) dims non-focused posters for a spotlight effect — driven by a per-card `@FocusState` in the file-private `PosterCardContent` (`LibraryPosterCard.swift`), no grid-level plumbing. Settings rows: `.tvSettingsFocusable()`. **Trait caveat**: a focused `Button` flips `UITraitCollection` to light-mode inside its label; `tvSettingsFocusable` takes `colorScheme` and injects on content + background shape — always pass `themeManager.darkModeEnabled ? .dark : .light`. **Hero `.focusSection()` rule**: any hero whose Play/More Info buttons sit in `.overlay(alignment: .bottomLeading)` of a tall `Color.clear` sizing block (Home + Library) needs `.focusSection()` on the buttons row AND the immediate row above (`tvTopBar` etc.) must also be a focus section — else up-presses get absorbed in the hero bounds.
- **RULE — tvOS has exactly TWO focus levels, and their numbers live in `CinemaTVFocus` (`TVButtonStyles.swift`), never as literals in a style.** **Carte** — poster, still, tile, CTA: accent ring (`cardRingWidth`), accent halo over an ambient shadow, and it grows (`cardScale`). **Rangée / pastille** — settings row, filter chip, season tab, episode zone, playlist row: accent stroke at the SAME width and opacity, plus `rowBrightness`, and it does NOT grow (a full-width row that scales visibly shifts its own content sideways; a chip that grows crowds its `FlowLayout` neighbours). Four unrelated treatments used to coexist — the visible symptom was a settings row (1.5 pt @ 0.8) accusing focus far more faintly than a filter chip (2 pt @ 1.0) on the same screen. The settings-landing pill keeps its `accentContainer` fill, which IS its indicator, but its motion and elevation are the card level's. A new focusable control picks one of the two; it does not invent a third.
- **RULE — tvOS layout metrics come from `CinemaTVLayout` (in `AdaptiveLayout.swift`, its iOS counterpart), never a bare literal in an `#if os(tvOS)` branch**: page margin, hero heights, card widths, the 6 × 32 grid, CTA widths, prose measure, title-logo box. They were literals repeated across seven screens, which is how the page margin came to be 112 pt on Library / Search / Home / the fiche and 48 pt on Favourites / Watch History — neighbouring screens with visibly different cards and gutters. These are RAW POINTS on purpose: `CinemaScale` scales *type*, and a hero that grew with the font-size slider would push its own buttons off screen. Added 2026-09-09 after a design pass on device: `actionRowHeight` (80 — the fiche's Play CTA and its accessories share it), `accessoryButtonWidth` (150), `formMaxWidth` (1400 — the settings-style column of every cover: Servers, Privacy, Connected devices, Server help, Discovery) and `personPortraitSize` (280).
- **RULE — every tvOS full-screen cover wears ONE chrome** (reference: `WatchedHistoryScreen.tvHeader`): a header row — title in `CinemaFont.headline(.large)`, `Spacer`, an **accent** `CinemaButton` Done/Cancel at `CinemaTVLayout.ctaWidth` — padded `pagePadding` / `spacing8` top / `spacing5` bottom with `.focusSection()`, content below at `pagePadding`, `.onExitCommand { dismiss() }`. Never `.toolbar` / `.navigationTitle` on tvOS (an empty pill and no title), never a round unfocusable `xmark`, never a ghost Done. Before the pass five dismiss idioms and four header margins coexisted; `LicensesView` had no exit at all on tvOS and `UserSwitchSheet` drew a toolbar pill. Adopted by Licences, Switch account, Quick Connect (both sheets), Episode overview, Add to playlist, « Lire sur… », Servers, Privacy, Connected devices, Server help, Discovery. **Do NOT put `.scrollClipDisabled()` on a cover's content `ScrollView` unless it holds cards that grow on focus** — with the clip off, scrolled rows draw straight under the header's buttons (seen on the Licences cover and on `LibrarySortFilterSheet`'s footer, whose decade chips scrolled under Réinitialiser / Appliquer). A cover whose only focusable is the header button must keep that `.focusSection()`, or the button is unreachable (Quick Connect).
- **RULE — tvOS heroes drift (`HeroBackdropImage`, `Components/HeroBackdropImage.swift`), they do NOT rotate.** iOS cycles up to five hero candidates with a crossfade; tvOS is excluded because auto-advancing focusable chrome fights the focus engine, which left its hero a single still that never changed for the life of the screen. The drift is 1.08 over 24 s, autoreversing — nothing focusable lives inside the backdrop, and it is far too slow to read as motion while the user is looking at a card. The hero clips, so the scaled edges never escape. Gated on `motionEffectsEnabled`, which is precisely what that setting is for. Carried by `HomeScreen.heroSection` and `LibraryHeroSection`. **The drift is a Core Animation `CABasicAnimation` on a UIKit image layer, never a SwiftUI `.repeatForever`**: SwiftUI ticks such an animation on the MAIN thread every frame, and the SwiftUI version kept the idle Home at ~1,760 main-thread samples per 15 s on the Apple TV 4K (A15) — re-committing and re-rasterising the hero's text — against 36 once the render server ran it (lot 8, 2026-09-26). The same holds for any new AMBIENT animation (one that runs while the user does nothing): Core Animation, or it does not ship. **A focus-coupled hero swap — the backdrop following the focused Continue Watching card — is deliberately NOT built**: it couples a page-level surface to a rail's focus state, which is the shape every focus bug in this app has had.
- **RULE — `ContentRow`'s horizontal padding is platform-dependent** (`CinemaTVLayout.pagePadding` on tvOS, `spacing6` elsewhere): a rail is one page element among others, and at a fixed 32 pt its header and first card sat visibly indented out of the column the hero, grid and top bar share. Its "View All" button carries a chip shape + `TVFilterChipButtonStyle` — bare text was the one focusable control on Home left with the system's own focus chrome.
- **iOS focus**: `.cinemaFocus()` (accent border + shadow).
- **CinemaButton styles**: `.accent` = primary CTAs (saturated `accentContainer` + `onAccentContainer` label — see the RULE above). `.primary` = neutral gradient. `.ghost` = secondary (Retry, Clear Filters). `.destructive` = `errorContainer` fill + `onErrorContainer` label, **irreversible actions only** (`DestructiveConfirmSheet`'s confirm button, and nothing else). **RULE — a destructive CTA is coloured by its STYLE, never by `.tint(...)` on the outside**: `CinemaButton` draws its own background and uses `.buttonStyle(.plain)`, so a tint modifier is silently inert — which is how the app's single most destructive control shipped in neutral grey (the sheet carried a `.tint(CinemaColor.error)` that had never rendered). Tonal rather than saturated, because it must read in both modes: `error` itself is a light salmon in dark mode, where a white label on it fails contrast outright.
- **Motion Effects**: `motionEffectsEnabled` env key = **`MotionEffects.isEnabled(appToggle:systemReduceMotion:)`** — the app's `@AppStorage("motionEffects")` AND NOT the system's Reduce Motion, combined ONCE at the `AppNavigation` root (`@Environment(\.accessibilityReduceMotion)`). When off, all `.animation()` → nil, the iOS hero carousel stops, the tvOS Ken Burns drift and every `symbolEffect` pulse go still — none of them needed a change, they already read the env key. **RULE — animations read `\.motionEffectsEnabled`, never the raw `@AppStorage` key** (`SettingsScreen` renamed its storage `motionEffectsSetting` — the row's binding — and reads the env value as `motionEffects`). The two readers with no environment go through `MotionEffects.isEnabledNow` (`UIAccessibility.isReduceMotionEnabled`): the rainbow accent tick (`ThemeManager`, nudged on either switch from `AppNavigation`'s `onChange`s) and the sign-in success dwell (`LoginViewModel`). The Motion Effects row keeps the app's own preference and, while Reduce Motion is on, says the system wins (`settings.motionEffects.systemOverride`, visible subtitle + VoiceOver hint, both platforms). Locked by `MotionEffectsTests`.
- **Haptics SSOT** (`DesignSystem/Haptics.swift`, `@MainActor` enum): ALL app haptics route through it — single owner of `UIFeedbackGenerator`, collapses to no-op on tvOS. `tap` (light impact — toggles), `success`/`warning`/`error` (notification — toasts), `selection` (info toasts). Exception: the VLC player HUD owns its own feedback in the presenter files.
- **VoiceOver on custom toggles** (`SettingsRowHelpers.swift`): `CinemaToggleIndicator` is visual-only — every toggle row collapses to one element via `.accessibilityElement(children: .ignore)` + `.accessibilityLabel` + `.accessibilityValue(a11y.toggle.on/off)` + `.accessibilityAddTraits(.isToggle)` + an `.accessibilityAction` that toggles and fires `Haptics.tap()`. New custom toggles must follow the same pattern.
- Platform-adaptive via `#if os(tvOS)` or `horizontalSizeClass`.

## Toasts & Empty states

- `ToastCenter` (`@Observable`, injected at `AppNavigation` root) — single-toast queue, auto-dismiss. `ToastOverlay` top-anchored glass pill. API `.success/.error/.info(_:message:duration:)`. **Use for action feedback and recoverable errors. NOT for critical errors needing a decision — use `UIAlertController`.**
- **RULE — a toast is ANNOUNCED to VoiceOver by `ToastCenter.show`, not by the overlay** (`AccessibilityNotification.Announcement`, title then message — `ToastCenter.announcement(for:)`; audit 2026-09-22, U3). VoiceOver said nothing about « Ajouté aux favoris », « Marqué comme vu » or any error. Under VoiceOver the toast also stays up `effectiveDuration` (≥ 6 s, twice the sighted duration). Its text is Dynamic Type (`dynamicLabel` / `dynamicBody`) and its ✕ is a 44 pt target pulled back into the pill's padding. Locked by `ToastAnnouncementTests`.
- `EmptyStateView` (icon + title + optional subtitle + action). Used by Home (all empty), filtered library grid ("Clear filters" resets `LibrarySortFilterState()`), `UserSwitchSheet`.
- **Illustrated states** (`CinemaIllustration.swift`): four static vector motifs — `.emptyLibrary`, `.noResults`, `.offline`, `.noFavorites` — drawn from SwiftUI shapes on a 100-unit grid (accent halo + neutral `surfaceContainerHigh/Highest` tiles + one `themeManager.accent*` gesture, **no strokes, no raster assets**), sized `CinemaScale.pt(112)`, `accessibilityHidden`. Opt-in through `EmptyStateView(illustration:)` / `ErrorStateView(illustration:)`; `nil` (default) keeps the SF Symbol, so untouched call sites look exactly as before. Adopted at Home all-empty, Search « Aucun résultat », Search's network error and the Favourites grid. **`.offline` goes only where the message is explicitly "can't reach the server"** (`search.error.network`) — a generic `error.generic` failure keeps the triangle, since the cloud would claim a cause the app doesn't know.

## Dynamic Type (iOS)

**RULE — there is NO root Dynamic Type cap; a surface that cannot follow the user's text size caps ITSELF through `.layoutBoundDynamicType()`** (`CinemaDynamicType.swift`, cap still `.accessibility2`). `AppNavigation` carried `.dynamicTypeSize(.xSmall ... .accessibility2)` on the whole tree, justified as protecting "hero titles and the tab bar". Both halves were wrong. It punished the wrong people — a user on one of the three largest accessibility sizes got a SMALLER layout than they chose on every screen, the reading ones included (a synopsis, the episode-overview sheet, the help sheet, the licences), which is exactly where the setting earns its keep. And it protected surfaces that need no protection: the Home and library heroes, the fiche title and every poster card size their type through `CinemaScale.pt`, which reads the app's own `uiScale` and **never** Dynamic Type, so lifting the cap cannot move them by a point. What actually scales is the native tab-bar / sidebar labels (`MainTabView` sets no font on them — the one surface that really collapses, and now the one that carries a local cap), native `List`/`Form` chrome, and `CinemaFont.dynamic*` text. Carriers of the local cap: `MainTabView`'s labels, `HomeScreen.heroSection`, `LibraryHeroSection`, `MediaDetailScreen.backdropSection`, `MediaDetailEpisodeCard`, `CastCircle`, `VideoPlayerView`. **Apply it as high as the fixed geometry reaches and NO higher** — a hero's synopsis and the sheet a card's « voir plus » opens sit outside it and scale all the way up.
- **Reading text follows Dynamic Type** (audit 2026-09-22): the iOS settings screens (`dynamicLabel`, `dynamicSystem(_:weight:relativeTo:)` for the category titles), `EmptyStateView` (`dynamicHeadline` + `dynamicBody`), `ErrorStateView` and the toasts. Section headers carry `.isHeader` (and read in natural case — `iOSSettingsSectionHeader`, the Privacy / Profile / sort-sheet headers, the empty-state title); a selected chip, sort row, season tab, accent swatch or language button carries `.isSelected`. The accent swatches read their colour's name (`AccentOption.nameKey` → `accent.name.*`) and fill equal 44 pt-tall slots; the tvOS accent and language rows are ONE element with the current choice as their value (accent: adjustable). The iOS hero carousel does not rotate under VoiceOver (`accessibilityVoiceOverEnabled`, WCAG 2.2.2). The watched check is `onSurface` on a `surface` disc — a white mark on the light-mode `#F7F7F8` disc was invisible.
- **RULE — `CinemaFont.dynamicBodyLayoutBound` is what bounds TEXT inside a capped surface, not the modifier.** `.layoutBoundDynamicType()` bounds SwiftUI's environment, while `CinemaFont.dynamic*` asks `UIFontMetrics` for the SYSTEM preferred size and never consults the environment — so a `.dynamicTypeSize(...)` ancestor does not clamp it and text in a fixed-width card would grow past its box regardless. The bound is a MULTIPLE of the app-scaled base (`CinemaDynamicType.layoutBoundMaxGrowth`, 1.35), so `uiScale` still applies underneath. Consumer: `MediaDetailEpisodeCard`'s 3-line overview. Everywhere the text owns its space, keep plain `dynamicBody` (there was a `dynamicBodyLarge`; it never had a call site and was deleted on 2026-09-10).
- **RULE — under VoiceOver the VLC player's HUD never auto-hides, the magic tap is play / pause and the escape gesture closes** (`scheduleHideControls` returns while `UIAccessibility.isVoiceOverRunning`, `voiceOverStatusDidChangeNotification` brings the HUD back, `accessibilityPerformMagicTap` → `playPauseTapped`, iOS `accessibilityPerformEscape` → `closePlayer(.user)` so a Watch Together session still asks; `view.accessibilityViewIsModal` keeps the app drawn behind the `.overFullScreen` presentation out of reach; audit 2026-09-22, U6). A HUD that fades every 4 s takes the element being swiped to away from under the user. The iOS top cluster is 44 pt tall (the ✕ 44 × 44) — it was ~31 pt; the width stays tighter so the five icons fit beside the title in portrait.
- **RULE — an iOS settings row with a control beside its label goes through `SettingsRowAdaptiveLayout` (`Shared/Screens/Settings/SettingsRowHelpers.swift`)**, which puts the control UNDER the label at the accessibility text sizes. Side by side, a fixed-width control — a toggle, the FR / EN pills, a `Stepper` whose own « 100% » grows with the text and is `fixedSize` — left the label a sliver, and it broke mid-word (« Tail/le/du/tex/te », « mouvem/ent », recette 2026-09-23). Carried by `iOSToggleRow` (hence every toggle catalogue) and the Appearance page's custom rows. A single word wider than the screen (« d'accentuation » at AX5) still breaks; no layout can prevent that.
- **RULE — every icon-only control of the VLC HUD reads its VoiceOver label from `PlayerHUDAccessibility`** (`Shared/Screens/VideoPlayer/PlayerAccessibility.swift`), built once per player from a `localize` closure so the labels are assertable without a live engine (`PlayerAccessibilityTests` builds it from the real fr / en bundles, never touching the app-wide language a parallel test reads). Two were wrong before it existed: the iOS ±10 s buttons announced « Passer l'intro » / « Passer le générique » — the skip-SEGMENT keys, a different control entirely — and **play/pause carried no label at all**. Its label follows the glyph (`setPlayPauseIcon(playing:)` re-sets it: a button drawn ⏸ is the one that pauses). The spinner is `isAccessibilityElement = true` with `.updatesFrequently` while staying `isUserInteractionEnabled = false` (accessibility and hit-testing are independent, and the no-hit-test rule is load-bearing — see the decorative-overlay RULE); without a label the whole open window reads as an empty black screen. The scrub bar's `accessibilityValue` is written by `writeTimeLabels`, the SOLE label writer, so it cannot drift from what is on screen, and it is WORDS — `PlayerTimeFormat.spoken(_:using:)` + a `DateComponentsFormatter` built once per player in the APP's language — because VoiceOver reads `12:04` as a time of day. The chapter strip's current chip carries `.selected` (`markCurrentChapter`, fed by `writeTimeLabels` through the pure `PlayerChapterSelection.currentIndex`; lot 9). **Still not done**: an on-device Accessibility Inspector pass.

## Image Patterns (all RULE)

- `ImageURLBuilder` → `/Items/{id}/Images/{type}`. **Backdrop sizing**: use `ImageURLBuilder.screenPixelWidth` — never hardcode `1920`.
- **A user's avatar is `/UserImage?userId=&tag=`, never `/Users/{id}/Images/Primary`** (`ImageURLBuilder.userImageURL`): the old route is absent from the 12.0 spec — the SDK only generates `Paths.getUserImage` — although it is still ROUTED on a 12.0 server (measured 2026-09-10: 400 for a bogus id, where an absent route answers 404), so this was hygiene and no avatar was ever broken. No `ServerVersion` gate: `/UserImage` exists from the 10.9 floor. It stays hand-built (Nuke sends it, and it must keep a sub-path-hosted server's base path) and keeps `maxWidth`/`quality`, which the server's image pipeline reads here exactly as on the old route even though the SDK's typed parameters model only `userId`/`tag`/`format`.
- **RULE — always pass the `tag:` cache-buster to `imageURL(...)`/`imageURLRaw(...)`**: without it the image URL is byte-identical across server-side poster/backdrop edits, so Nuke (keyed by URL) serves the stale image forever — a metadata/poster change is invisible until reinstall, and `clearCache()`/"Refresh Catalogue" don't help (they clear the JSON cache, not Nuke's). Pass `tag: item.primaryImageTagValue` for `.primary`, `tag: item.backdropImageTagValue` for `.backdrop` (helpers in `BaseItemDto+Metadata.swift`; backdrop helper mirrors `backdropItemID`'s parent-first resolution), `person.primaryImageTag` for cast. Every new live image call site must thread the tag. **Chapter thumbnails and now-playing artwork also carry a tag** — since both now ride Nuke's shared 500 MB disk cache via `AuthenticatedImageFetch` (see Player-image caching RULE below), an untagged URL there is just as stale-prone as a poster/backdrop; `ChapterController`/`VLCStreamPresenter` pass each chapter's `imageTag`, and `NowPlayingInfoController` passes `fullItem.primaryImageTagValue` (see its enrichment-sequencing note). **Trickplay tiles stay deliberately tag-less** — regenerating trickplay changes the manifest/`mediaSourceId`, which already busts the URL, and the player re-fetches the manifest every session anyway.
- **Image cache**: `AppNavigation.init()` configures `ImagePipeline.shared` with 500 MB disk + explicit `ImageCache` `costLimit = 256 MB` (Nuke's ~100 MB default evicts mid-render on tvOS — 4K backdrops decode to 4–8 MB each).
- **Prefetch (`PosterPrefetcher`, Shared/ViewModels)**: Home + Library warm card images via Nuke `ImagePrefetcher` after each data load. **RULE — prefetched URLs must be byte-identical to the consuming card's request** (same `maxWidth` AND `tag`) — Nuke keys on the URL, a near-miss warms nothing. **The same rule binds every non-card surface that shows an image the app has already downloaded**, and three sites were off by a width: the tvOS HUD poster asked for 240 where every card asks 300 (a fresh download mid-playback), and the Watch Together lobby's backdrop asked `screenPixelWidth` where the fiche behind it asks `backdropPixelWidth` (uncapped, so a second download on an iPad Pro). Home's prefetch also simply **omitted** the playlists / collections / upcoming rails, so those cards downloaded on first scroll while every older row was warm. Posters = primary/300, continue-watching = backdrop/600. Call `reset()` before a full catalogue refresh so refreshed tags re-prefetch.
- **Backdrop fallback**: `item.backdropItemID` (→ `parentBackdropItemID ?? seriesID ?? id`). **No-backdrop placeholder**: gate on `item.hasBackdropImage` (NOT `backdropItemID` — always non-nil) → `BackdropFallbackView` (centered `film` symbol over `surfaceContainerLow` + accent radial, under `CinemaGradient.heroOverlay`). Wired in `MediaDetailScreen.backdropSection`/`HomeScreen.heroSection`/`LibraryHeroSection`.
- **Always `CinemaLazyImage`** — never `LazyImage` directly. Card containers: `Color.clear` + `.aspectRatio()` + `.frame(maxWidth: .infinity)` + `.overlay { CinemaLazyImage }` + `.clipped()`. **RULE — a full-bleed hero is a `Color.clear` sizing driver with the backdrop, gradient and title as `.overlay`s, never a `ZStack`** (`HomeScreen.heroSection`, `LibraryHeroSection`, `MediaDetailScreen.backdropSection`): a `.fill` `CinemaLazyImage` reports its FILLED height (width × 9/16) even under `.frame(maxWidth:.infinity, maxHeight:.infinity)`, so a ZStack around it outgrows a clamped hero, is centred in it and clipped — the bottom-aligned title is cut. Measured 2026-09-25 on the fiche, iPad 13″ landscape: ≈ 774 pt of image against a 460 pt hero, logo halved and genres gone (portrait lost only the genres, which is why it went unnoticed). Overlays cannot grow their parent. Outer container `LazyVStack(alignment: .leading)`.
- **RULE — a clipped `CinemaLazyImage` container MUST carry `.contentShape(Rectangle())`, right after `.clipped()`/`.clipShape(...)`**: `CinemaLazyImage` scales to **fill**, so any source whose aspect ratio differs from its box overflows it — a 16:9 episode still stands in a 2:3 poster slot on the search and library grids, sticking out **~94 pt on each side** of a 112 pt card. `clipped()` and `clipShape()` clip the **drawing only**; the overflow stays hit-testable, so the next card in the grid — drawn after, hence on top — swallows long presses aimed at the previous card's right half and opens **its** context menu. Measured before the fix (search "tom"): a touch at x=96, inside the card spanning x=16→128.7, built the menu of the card at x=144.7; every result reproduced identically on pre-merge `fd1a817`, so this was long-standing, not a 1.2.0 regression. **The shape belongs on the clipped CONTAINER, not inside `CinemaLazyImage`** — verified: placing it on the image itself does not fix anything, because the container's clip is what defines the visible box. Carried by `PosterCard`, `LibraryPosterCard` and `WideCard`; any new card that clips a filled image needs it too. **This is also the mechanism behind the long-standing "context menu acts on the wrong item" defect, established 2026-08-28 by a paired control** (Watched History, two `Arrow` episodes, press at x=96 with card A on x 16–129 and card B on x 146–256): with the shape, the press resolves to **A**; with it commented out and rebuilt, to **B** — and it is visible to the eye, the finger is on A, which stays put, and B is what lifts. **The decisive negative result: without the fix the menu AND the preview both name B.** They read the same `item` on the same modifier, so a menu/preview disagreement is structurally impossible — which is exactly why two probe campaigns that went looking for one found nothing and left the defect open. It also reconciles the original report: the pixels under the finger already belonged to B, so the preview "showed the pressed card"; "Go to series" appeared because B is an episode; and the add-to-playlist header named B, a title never yet seen on screen, which read as a *third* designation. Method that settled it, when a probe could not: comment out the suspect line, rebuild, replay the SAME coordinate, compare.
- **RULE — a `Button` whose label is a full-width row (`HStack` + `Spacer`) also needs `.contentShape(Rectangle())`, for the opposite reason**: a `Button` only receives touches where its label actually DRAWS, and neither a `Spacer` nor padding draws anything — so a row whose unselected background is `Color.clear` is tappable on its **label alone**. The failure is asymmetric and therefore misleading: the *selected* row keeps working because its accent fill (even at 0.08) is a real colour, so the bug only bites the rows the user is trying to reach. Hit `LibrarySortFilterSheet.sortRow` (≈30 pt tappable out of a 370 pt row); the sheet's other controls were spared only because chips and the « Non vus » row always carry a real background. Symptom to recognise: a synthetic or real tap that lands nowhere while *neighbouring controls on the same screen* respond normally — suspect the hit area, not the input.
- **RULE — a tappable settings row puts `iOSSettingsRow` INSIDE its label (`Button { … } label: { iOSSettingsRow { … } }`), never a `Button` inside `iOSSettingsRow`.** The container carries the `contentShape(Rectangle())` over its padding; a button nested inside it gains nothing from that shape and answered on its icon, text and chevron only — every navigation row of Réglages and the admin did. Toggle rows follow the same shape: the whole row is the button and `CinemaToggleIndicator` is plain content (`iOSToggleRow`, `SettingsRowAdaptiveLayout`'s `control:` slot), whose off track is `outlineVariant` — `surfaceContainerHighest` is the glass panel's own fill and drew an off toggle as a lone white knob.
- **RULE — a poster card's watched check / progress bar comes from `MediaCardStatus` (`ProgressBarView.swift`) and is applied with `.mediaCardStatusOverlay(_:)` on the CLIPPED artwork box.** It derives the state through `CardPlayTargetResolver.isResumable`, the same SSOT the card's own play menu reads, so the badge cannot contradict the menu sitting on top of it: a residual position on an item already marked played is the check, never a bar. Below 1 % reads as untouched (a sliver of bar looks like a rendering artefact); no runtime means no bar, while the played check still stands (a series folder carries no position of its own). Carried by `PosterCard` (via its `status:` parameter, default `.none`) and by `LibraryPosterCard`, which derives its own. **Deliberately NOT passed** on `WatchedHistoryScreen` (every item there is watched — a check on every card is noise) or `LibraryFolderBrowseScreen` (folders). **Known staleness, accepted**: surfaces that refetch on a tier-2 notification (the four paginated grids, the fiche's similar row, Home's favourites row) update at once; rails that do not (Home's genre rows and Recently Added, the library browse layout's genre rows, Search results) keep their badge until the next full reload. Refreshing them would mean either the 8-genre fan-out or a `reset()` that costs the user their scroll position — both proscribed by the two-tier RULE. Locked by `MediaCardStatusTests`.
- **PosterCard title alignment**: hidden `Text("M\nM").hidden()` placeholder + actual title overlaid top-aligned → uniform row height.

## App Icons

- iOS: `Resources/Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.appiconset/` (1024² light/dark/tinted). tvOS: `App Icon & Top Shelf Image.brandassets/` (3-layer parallax + Top Shelf 1920×720 + Wide 2320×720). In-app logo: `AppLogo.imageset/`. Standalone source: `appIcon.png` at project root.
- **RULE — the iOS launch screen names a FIXED dark colour (`LaunchBackground.colorset`, `UILaunchScreen.UIColorName` in `project.yml`), never an empty dict and never a dynamic colorset.** The key itself is mandatory from the iOS 27 SDK on (a build carrying none is rejected), but the empty dict it used to be resolves to `systemBackground` — i.e. **white** on a light-mode phone. `darkMode` is a *per-app* setting (default `true`) that the system appearance never sees, so a user whose phone is in light mode got a white flash before a dark app whatever they had chosen in Cinemax. Measured on 2026-09-01 with a paired control on one iPhone 17, system in light, the app frozen at launch via `simctl launch --wait-for-debugger` (the launch window is far too short for a screenshot race — three tries caught only the rendered app): `UIColorName` ⇒ **`#0E0E0E` full screen**, the same bundle with `UILaunchScreen` put back to `{}` ⇒ **pure white**. A dynamic light/dark colorset is the trap here, not the fix: it follows the *system* appearance, so it would put the white screen back in precisely the case being fixed. The value duplicates `CinemaColor.surface`'s dark component **by necessity** — nothing Swift has run yet, so the theme cannot be consulted; it is the one legitimate place for a hardcoded colour outside `CinemaGlassTheme.swift`. Accepted trade: a user who turned dark mode *off* gets a dark→light transition, which is both rarer (dark is the default) and gentler than white→dark. tvOS deliberately keeps `UILaunchScreen: {}` — the requirement is iOS/iPadOS-only and a tvOS app launches over the system's own background.
- **RULE — text never uses `outline` / `outlineVariant`; tertiary text is `CinemaColor.onSurfaceMuted`** (lot 9). Those two are STROKE tokens: as card subtitles they read 2.0:1 (light) and 4.2:1 (dark), and `WideCard`'s detail line ~1.4:1 in light. `onSurfaceMuted` clears 4.5:1 on `surface` in both modes (4.8 / 5.8).
- **RULE — toasts live in a separate `UIWindow` above the app's (`ToastWindow.swift`, `windowLevel = .normal + 1`)**, hosted once from the root by `ToastWindowHost` — never `ToastOverlay` in the view tree: an in-tree overlay draws under every sheet, cover and the UIKit player, so a toast raised from a modal was only seen once it closed. The window never becomes key, passes every touch outside the pill's REPORTED frame through (`ToastPassthroughWindow.toastFrame`), takes no input on tvOS, re-applies the app's dark/light choice on `themeManager.colorScheme` (NOT `darkModeEnabled`, which Observation does not track), and its root `ToastHostingController` answers status-bar / home-indicator questions with the app window's top controller — a plain hosting controller would say « shown » over the VLC player (review of 142db6e, lot 9). The toast title is capped at 6 lines.
